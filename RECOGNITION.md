# StygNox Recognition Policy

StygNox should make contribution provenance visible rather than allowing meaningful work to disappear into commit history.

The project recognises that valuable contributions take several forms. A person may originate an idea, shape a design, implement it, test it, document it, review it, find a defect, or provide evidence that materially changes the result.

## What should be credited

For material features and changes, maintainers should record the relevant roles where practical:

- **Originated by** — person or people who first proposed the material idea;
- **Designed by** — contributors who materially shaped the solution;
- **Implemented by** — contributors who wrote or integrated the implementation;
- **Tested/validated by** — contributors who materially validated the work;
- **Reviewed by** — contributors who performed material technical or design review;
- **Documentation by** — contributors who created substantial supporting documentation.

One person may hold several roles.

## Idea/Feature Registry provenance

Where the StygNox Idea/Feature Registry is used, a material item should preserve, where available:

```text
Originated by:
Source / provenance:
Design contributors:
Implementation contributors:
Review / validation contributors:
Issue / work item:
Released in:
```

Credit should travel with the item through planning, implementation and release rather than being reconstructed afterwards.

## CONTRIBUTORS.md

`CONTRIBUTORS.md` is the durable human-readable record of people who have made accepted material contributions. Contributors may choose a real name, handle, organisation/affiliation where appropriate, or opt out of public listing.

## CREDITS.md

`CREDITS.md` records notable feature and idea provenance. It may include people whose contribution was primarily ideation, research, design, testing, documentation or review rather than source code.

## Release notes

Significant releases should credit the originator and implementation contributors for material features where that provenance is known and publication is appropriate.

## Recognition is not ownership of an abstract idea

This policy is about acknowledgement and project history. It does not state that a contributor owns an abstract feature idea, concept, method or suggestion merely because they proposed it. Copyright and other intellectual-property rights depend on applicable law and the material actually created.

## Corrections

If a contributor believes a material credit is missing or wrong, they may request a correction through the Project's normal contribution or issue process. Maintainers should correct clear provenance errors promptly.

## No forced publicity

A contributor may ask not to be publicly credited or may request that a reasonable pseudonym/handle be used instead.
